Both airports have published September. Vienna had **2,910,597** passengers, 7.5 % fewer than a year ago. Bratislava had **498,957**, 89 % more. For the first nine months of the year Vienna has **23,478,801** passengers (−4.5 %) and Bratislava **3,693,231** (+99 %).

## It is not a simple transfer

From January to September Vienna lost **1.09 million** passengers and Bratislava gained **1.84 million**. So Bratislava grew by more than Vienna lost. In a pure transfer of passengers we would expect similar numbers. Part of Bratislava's growth therefore does not come from Vienna (new flights, people from other airports, new demand), but where exactly it comes from, these data can't say.

## Vienna: below last year every month since April

Since April, Vienna has been below last year's level every month (year over year from −4.7 % to −8.2 %). September (−7.5 %) is one of the weaker months. The airport explains it with lower capacity of low-cost airlines and the Middle East crisis. Wizz Air, which ended its Vienna operations on 15 March 2026, carried 1,832,033 passengers there in 2025 (5.6 % of the airport) and only 165,956 (1.2 %) in the first half of 2026, according to the airport's results.

## Bratislava: growth continues, but it is changing

Bratislava's year-over-year growth was +127 % in January, +81 % in June and +89 % in September. In absolute terms September is somewhat lower than the three summer months (498,957 against 625,742 in August), which is normal seasonality. More interesting will be growth from November: Wizz Air opened its Bratislava base on 14 November 2025, so from next month the comparison is against a period when it had already grown.

## Airfares in September

According to Eurostat, airfares in Slovakia in September were 55.8 % lower than a year ago, in Austria 12.0 % higher and on EU average 5.6 % higher. Details are in the article on [prices in the dashboard](/posts/ceny-letenky-v-dashboarde/).

## A note on the data

On 12 October Bratislava Airport published only a total for the first nine months, not a separate September figure. So my scraper calculated the month: the total (3,693,231) minus January to August (3,194,274) is 498,957. Eurostat later gave the same number. The rule is written into the code: it derives a month only when it is missing and all the earlier months are known. For Vienna I use the airport's own figures, because Eurostat counts 1 to 2 % higher.

This is a summary in numbers, not an explanation of causes. The causes of the shift in capacity (prices, charges, taxes, airline strategy) can't be proved from these data, which is why I don't claim them in the text.
